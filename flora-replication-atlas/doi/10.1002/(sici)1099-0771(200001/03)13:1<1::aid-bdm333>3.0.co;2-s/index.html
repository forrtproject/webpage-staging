The affect heuristic in judgments of risks and benefits

Melissa L. Finucane, Ali Alhakami, Paul Slovic, Stephen M. Johnson

Journal of Behavioral Decision Making, 2000. DOI 10.1002/(sici)1099-0771(200001/03)13:1<1::aid-bdm333>3.0.co;2-s

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 1 replication of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 successful. The earliest on record is from 2021.

Replications

  • Risky Therefore Not Beneficial: Replication and Extension of Finucane et al.'s (2000) Affect Heuristic Experiment, Efendić et al. (2021). Social Psychological and Personality Science. Outcome recorded: successful. In two well-powered studies, using a more stringent analytic strategy, we replicated the original finding. We observed a strong negative relationship between judgments of risks and benefits across three technologies, although we do find that there was no change in risks when highlighting low benefits.
