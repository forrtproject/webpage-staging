Paranormal and Religious Believers Are More Prone to Illusory Face Perception than Skeptics and Non‐believers

Tapani Riekki, Marjaana Lindeman, Marja Aleneff, Anni Halme, Antti Nuortimo

Applied Cognitive Psychology, 2012. DOI 10.1002/acp.2874

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 1 replication of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 successful. The earliest on record is from 2017.

Replications

  • Being Watched by Anthropomorphized Objects Affects Charitable Donation in Religious People, Miyazaki (2017). Japanese Psychological Research. Outcome recorded: successful. These results suggest that the religious participants were more prone to perceive a face in everyday objects than less-religious participants, which is consistent with a past finding (Riekki et al., 2013).
