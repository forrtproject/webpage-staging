  • Default effects: Replications and Extensions of Johnson & Goldstein (2003) and Johnson, Bellman, & Lohse (2002)‎, Chandrashekar et al. (2022). OSF. Outcome recorded: mixed. We, however, failed to replicate the findings of Johnson et al. (2002)'s study; we did not find evidence for a default effect. We did, however, find a framing effect: participants who read a positively-framed scenario consented to receive health-related information at a higher rate than participants who read a negatively framed scenario.
