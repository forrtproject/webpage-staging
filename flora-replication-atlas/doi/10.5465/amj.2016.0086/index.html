The Paradox of Family Structure and Plans after Work: Why Single Childless Employees May Be the Least Absorbed at Work

Tracy L. Dumas, Jill E. Perry-Smith

Academy of Management Journal, 2018. DOI 10.5465/amj.2016.0086

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 1 replication and 1 reproduction of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 failed, 1 mixed. The earliest on record is from 2020.

Replications

  • Dumas AcaManageJourn 2018 5KrD - Gooty - New Data Replication, Gooty et al. (2020). OSF. Outcome recorded: failed. "With family structure, job level, age, and biological sex in the regression model, we found a non-significant relationship between family structure and work absorption (b = -0.015190, p = 0.85130). … The result did not replicate based on the SCORE replication criteria." Outcome read from OSF report. View paper

Reproductions

  • Dumas AcaManageJourn 2018 5KrD - Somo - Reproduction (with author data & code), Loomas et al. (2020). OSF. Outcome recorded: computationally reproducible, not checked. Computational: "Confirmed. The effect of being single and childless on work absorption was negative and significant... (b = -.31, SE = 0.13, p < .05)." Outcome read from Computational: Reproduction result. View paper
