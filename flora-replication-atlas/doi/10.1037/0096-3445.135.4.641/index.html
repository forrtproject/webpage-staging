Seeing the forest when entry is unlikely: Probability and the mental representation of events

Cheryl J. Wakslak, Yaacov Trope, Nira Liberman, Rotem Alony

Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 2006. DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.135.4.641

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 3 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 3 failed. The earliest on record is from 2020.

Replications

  • Subjective likelihood and the construal level of future events: A replication study of Wakslak, Trope, Liberman, and Alony (2006), Calderon et al. (2020). Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. Outcome recorded: failed. Contrary to the original empirical findings, we present 2 close replication attempts (N 115 and N 120; the original studies had N 20 and N 34) that failed to find the effect of likelihood on construal level. Bayesian analyses provided diagnostic support for the absence of an effect. Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • Subjective Probability and the Construal Level of Future Events: A Replication Study of Wakslak, Trope, Liberman, and Alony (2006), Calderon (2020). OSF. Outcome recorded: failed. Contrary to the original empirical findings, we present two close replication attempts (N = 115 and N = 120; the original studies had N = 20 and N = 34) which failed to find the effect of likelihood on construal level. Outcome read from the abstract.
  • Effects of Psychological Distance on Mental Abstraction: A Registered Report of Four Tests of Construal-Level Theory, Calderon et al. (2026). Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. Outcome recorded: failed. The current research presents strong evidence of weak or non-existent relationships between the four forms of psychological distance and mental abstraction, as operationalized in the current studies. Outcome read from conclusions. View paper
