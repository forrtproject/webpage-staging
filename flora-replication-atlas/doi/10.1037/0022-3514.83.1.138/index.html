The role of public exposure in moral and nonmoral shame and guilt

Richard H. Smith, J. Matthew Webster, W. Gerrod Parrott, Heidi L. Eyre

Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2002. DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.83.1.138

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 1 replication of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 failed. The earliest on record is from 2023.

Replications

  • Revisiting the Role of Public Exposure and Moral Beliefs on Feelings of Shame and Guilt: Replication Registered Report of Smith et al. (2002)'s Study 1, Zhang et al. (2023). Collabra: Psychology. Outcome recorded: failed. Our results suggest a failed replication: public exposure and moral belief influence both shame and guilt, so we cannot conclude that shame and guilt can be distinguished from each other solely based on public exposure, which diverges from the target article's main theory and findings.
