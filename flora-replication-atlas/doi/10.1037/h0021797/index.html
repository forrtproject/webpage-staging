Primary memory

Nancy C. Waugh, Donald A. Norman

Psychological Review, 1965. DOI 10.1037/h0021797

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 2 mixed. The earliest on record is from 2020.

Replications

  • A Replication of Waugh and Norman (1965) Primary Memory study, Poitras et al. (2020). The Quantitative Methods for Psychology. Outcome recorded: mixed. Both studies' results partially support Waugh & Norman's claim, as participants were more likely to recall a digit that was followed by fewer interfering items. Additionally, we observed an interaction involving presentation rate and interference, as participants performed best with a low amount of interfering items presented at 1 item per second. Outcome recorded: mixed. The findings of a direct replication effort revealed that recall performance decreased with increasing interfering items, although the pattern of decreased performance did not reflect the original study.
