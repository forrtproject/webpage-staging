Attention control, memory updating, and emotion regulation temporarily reduce the capacity for executive control

Brandon J. Schmeichel

Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 2007. DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.136.2.241

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 successful, 1 failed. The earliest on record is from 2011.

Replications

  • The stability of working memory: Do previous tasks influence complex span?, Healey et al. (2011). Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. Outcome recorded: successful. High-demand participants recalled an average of 54.3% {SD = 12.2) of the words, substantially fewer words than the 69.8% (SD = 11.4) recalled by low-demand participants, f(36) = 4.06, p < .001, ci = 1.35, replicating the Schmeichel (2007) finding. Outcome read from p. 576. View paper
  • No Evidence of the Ego-Depletion Effect across Task Characteristics and Individual Differences: A Pre-Registered Study, Lurquin et al. (2016). PLOS ONE. Outcome recorded: failed. Contrary to the ego- depletion hypothesis, participants in the depletion condition did not perform worse than con- trol participants on the subsequent self-control task, even after considering moderator vari- ables. Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
