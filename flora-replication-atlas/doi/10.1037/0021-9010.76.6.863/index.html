Models of supervisory job performance ratings

Walter C. Borman, Leonard A. White, Elaine D. Pulakos, Scott H. Oppler

Journal of Applied Psychology, 1991. DOI 10.1037/0021-9010.76.6.863

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 1 replication of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 successful. The earliest on record is from 2000.

Replications

  • Replication and Extension of Models of Supervisory Job Performance Ratings, Lance & Bennett, Jr. (2000). Human Performance. Outcome recorded: successful. Results indicated that (a) effects of ability and experience were linear, not interactive; (b) different conceptualizations of "experience" play somewhat different causal roles in the determination of job knowledge, job proficiency, and supervisory ratings; (c) general support for the mediational roles of job knowledge and job proficiency; and (d) supervisory ratings reflect both technical (task-based) and motivational (contextual) aspects of performance.
