Why people are reluctant to tempt fate

Jane L. Risen, Thomas Gilovich

Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2008. DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.95.2.293

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 4 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 3 failed, 1 successful. The earliest on record is from 2015.

Replications

  • Many Labs 2: Investigating Variation in Replicability Across Samples and Settings, Klein et al. (2018). Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. Outcome recorded: successful. This is consistent with the hypothesis that people believe tempting fate increases the likelihood of a negative outcome, though the effect size was less than half the effect size in the original study (d = 0.18, 95% CI = [0.14, 0.22], vs. original d = 0.39). Outcome read from the results. View paper
  • Many Labs 5: Testing Pre-Data-Collection Peer Review as an Intervention to Increase Replicability, Ebersole et al. (2020). Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. Outcome recorded: failed. 95% CI for the meta-analytic replication includes 0 under both the RP:P and Revised Protocols (effect not significant in the same direction as the original under either). Outcome read from coding note: Table 3. View paper
  • Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science, Collaboration (2015). Science. Outcome recorded: failed. Coded as not replicated: Replicate = 0 (the project's binary success indicator combining significance and direction). Outcome read from coding note: data file (osf.io). View paper
  • Eleven years of student replication projects provide evidence on the correlates of replicability in psychology, Boyce et al. (2023). Royal Society Open Science. Outcome recorded: failed. Our data failed to replicated both of these findings. Outcome read from p. 7. View paper
