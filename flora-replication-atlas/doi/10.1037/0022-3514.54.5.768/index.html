Inhibiting and facilitating conditions of the human smile: A nonobtrusive test of the facial feedback hypothesis

Fritz Strack, Leonard L. Martin, Sabine Stepper

Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 1988. DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.54.5.768

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 successful, 1 failed. The earliest on record is from 2016.

Replications

  • When both the original study and its failed replication are correct: Feeling observed eliminates the facial-feedback effect, Noah et al. (2018). Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. Outcome recorded: successful. According to the main hypothesis of this research, both analyses indicated that in the no-camera condition, as in the study by Strack et al. (1988), the participants in the teeth condition rated the cartoons as significantly more amusing than did participants in the lips condition (Mteeth = 5.75, SD = 1.35, Mlips = 4.92, SD = 1.46), t(162) = 2.48, p = .01, ηp2 = .038, 95% confidence limits: [.171–1.500], BF = 8.66. Outcome read from the results. View paper
  • Registered Replication Report, Wagenmakers et al. (2016). Perspectives on Psychological Science. Outcome recorded: failed. Our meta-analysis revealed a rating difference of 0.03 units with a 95% confidence interval ranging from −0.11 to 0.16. Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
