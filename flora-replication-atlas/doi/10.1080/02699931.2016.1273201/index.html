Facial redness, expression, and masculinity influence perceptions of anger and health

Steven G. Young, Christopher A. Thorstenson, Adam D. Pazda

Cognition and Emotion, 2016. DOI 10.1080/02699931.2016.1273201

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 1 replication of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 mixed. The earliest on record is from 2022.

Replications

  • The Red-Anger Effect: Is it Nothing More Than Demand Characteristics?, Wolf et al. (2022). Outcome recorded: statistically successful but flawed. In a preregistered close replication (Study 1, N = 40) conducted online, we observed the red-anger effect but were able to show that approximately one fifth of the participants were suspicious about the purpose of the study.
