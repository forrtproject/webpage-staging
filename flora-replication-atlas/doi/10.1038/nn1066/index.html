Perceiving numbers causes spatial shifts of attention

Martin H Fischer, Alan D Castel, Michael D Dodd, Jay Pratt

Nature Neuroscience, 2003. DOI 10.1038/nn1066

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 2 failed. The earliest on record is from 2014.

Replications

  • Registered Replication Report on Fischer, Castel, Dodd, and Pratt (2003), Colling et al. (2020). Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. Outcome recorded: failed. we conclude that we failed to replicate the effect reported by Fischer et al. Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • Number-induced shifts in spatial attention: a replication study, Zanolie & Pecher (2014). Frontiers in Psychology. Outcome recorded: failed. Our results provide little support for the idea that numbers shift spatial attention. Only in one of the two experiments in which participants processed number magnitude did participants respond faster to targets in congruent locations (left for low magnitudes and right for high magnitudes) than in incongruent locations.
