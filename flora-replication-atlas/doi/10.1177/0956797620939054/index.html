Fighting COVID-19 Misinformation on Social Media: Experimental Evidence for a Scalable Accuracy-Nudge Intervention

Gordon Pennycook, Jonathon McPhetres, Yunhao Zhang, Jackson G. Lu, David G. Rand

Psychological Science, 2020. DOI 10.1177/0956797620939054

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications and 1 reproduction of this study. Recorded outcomes: 2 mixed, 1 failed. The earliest on record is from 2021.

Replications

  • How Accurate Are Accuracy-Nudge Interventions? A Preregistered Direct Replication of Pennycook et al. (2020), Roozenbeek et al. (2021). Psychological Science. Outcome recorded: mixed. The first stage of the replication test ( n = 701) was unsuccessful ( p = .67). After collecting a second round of data (additional n = 882, pooled N = 1,583), we found a small but significant interaction between treatment condition and truth discernment (uncorrected p = .017; treatment: d = 0.14, control: d = 0.10). As in the target study, perceived headline accuracy correlated with treatment impact, so that treatment-group participants were less willing to share headlines… Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • Fighting the spread of COVID-19 misinformation in Kyrgyzstan, India, and the United States: How replicable are accuracy nudge interventions?, Gavin et al. (2022). Technology, Mind, and Behavior. Outcome recorded: mixed. The accuracy nudge's effect did not replicate in the Kyrgyzstan sample (n = 1,049). Results were mixed in India (n = 703) and the United States (n = 829); the nudge decreased willingness to share some misinformation Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
