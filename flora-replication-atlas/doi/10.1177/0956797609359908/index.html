What Counts as a Choice?

Krishna Savani, Hazel Rose Markus, N.V.R. Naidu, Satishchandra Kumar, Neha Berlia

Psychological Science, 2010. DOI 10.1177/0956797609359908

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 3 replications and 2 reproductions of this study. Recorded outcomes: 3 failed, 1 mixed, 1 successful. The earliest on record is from 2018.

Replications

  • Eleven years of student replication projects provide evidence on the correlates of replicability in psychology, Boyce et al. (2023). Royal Society Open Science. Outcome recorded: failed. Subjective replication success rating = 0 (failed). The replication report rated each sub-outcome on a 0-1 scale; the row is recorded as failed only when all sub-outcomes equal 0, successful only when all equal 1, and mixed for any value strictly between. Outcome read from coding note (github.com). View paper
  • Many Labs 2: Investigating Variation in Replicability Across Samples and Settings, Klein et al. (2018). Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. Outcome recorded: mixed. Though there was a significant main effect in the full sample, the absence of a significant main effect in this subsample, control- ling for importance, is inconsistent with the original finding that Indians are less likely to construe personal actions than interpersonal actions as choices. There was an interaction between condition and rating of the importance of the choice, with a pattern similar to that in the full sample. This moderation was not reported in the… Outcome read from p. 20. View paper
  • Savani PsychologSci 2010 88xa - Safin - New Data Replication, Safin et al. (2019). OSF. Outcome recorded: successful. "The H focal test (two-way ANOVA) confirms a significant interaction, F(1,444) = 19.51, p = 1.26 × 10⁻⁵ in the predicted direction, such that Indian participants had fewer clicks than Americans in the experimental 'Choice' condition, but not in the control condition. The results are remarkably similar to the original paper, not only yielding the same overall effect, but also nearly identical means for each cell." Outcome read from OSF report. View paper
