High in the hierarchy: How vertical location and judgments of leaders' power are interrelated

Steffen R. Giessner, Thomas W. Schubert

Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 2007. DOI 10.1016/j.obhdp.2006.10.001

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 mixed, 1 failed. The earliest on record is from 2018.

Replications

  • Power and Vertical Positions in an Organization Chart: A Pre-Registered Replication Report of Study 3a and a Modification of Study 1a, Giessner & Schubert (2007), Giessner & Schubert (2019). Collabra: Psychology. Outcome recorded: mixed. Our current preregistered Study 1 confirms original Study 3a of Giessner and Schubert (2007). Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • Many Labs 2: Investigating Variation in Replicability Across Samples and Settings, Klein et al. (2018). Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. Outcome recorded: failed. This result does not support the hypoth- esis that perceived power is higher with greater vertical distance. The replication effect was in the same direc- tion as, but much smaller than, the original (d = 0.03, 95% CI = [−0.01, 0.08], vs. original d = 0.55). Outcome read from p. 18. View paper
