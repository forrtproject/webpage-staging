When errors do not matter: Weakening belief in intentional control impairs cognitive reaction to errors

Davide Rigoni, Hélène Wilquin, Marcel Brass, Boris Burle

Cognition, 2013. DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2013.01.009

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 2 failed. The earliest on record is from 2020.

Replications

  • Are post-error adjustments influenced by beliefs in free will? A failure to replicate Rigoni, Wilquin, Brass and Burle, 2013, Eben et al. (2020). Royal Society Open Science. Outcome recorded: failed. we did not find any difference in post-error slowing between the anti-free will and control groups. Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • Are post-error adjustments influenced by beliefs in free will? A failure to replicate Rigoni, Wilquin, Brass and Burle, 2013, unknown authors (2020). OSF. Outcome recorded: failed. However, we were not able to reproduce the finding that reduced belief in free will leads to reduced PES. Even though we did not reach our aimed BF of larger than 10 or smaller than 0.1, our BF revealed substantial evidence in favour of the null hypothesis. We, therefore, were not able to replicate the central finding that the Crick manipulation of belief in free will influences post-error adjustment behaviour. Outcome read from the discussion.
