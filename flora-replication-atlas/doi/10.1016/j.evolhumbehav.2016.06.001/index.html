Life history strategies and psychopathology: the faster the life strategies, the more symptoms of psychopathology

Jessie E. Hurst, Phillip S. Kavanagh

Evolution and Human Behavior, 2017. DOI 10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2016.06.001

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 1 replication and 1 reproduction of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 failed, 1 mixed. The earliest on record is from 2019.

Replications

  • Hurst EvoHumanBehavior 2017 yypJ - Röer - New Data Replication, Röer et al. (2019). OSF. Outcome recorded: failed. "For stage 2 data collection (with 25 additional participants, analytic N = 68), we found a significant correlation between life history (i.e., Mini-K total life history score) and psychopathology (i.e., index of psychopathology) with age as a covariate, r = .367, p = .002, ƒ² = .16, again in the opposite direction of that in the original study. Thus, in the final sample, the replication was unsuccessful according to the SCORE criteria." Outcome read from OSF report. View paper

Reproductions

  • Reproduction (with author data & code): Hurst & Kavanagh (2017, Evolution and Human Behavior), Röer et al. (2020). OSF. Outcome recorded: computationally reproducible, not checked. Computational: "the focal variable p value and the focal variable coefficient value were the same as in the original study. Thus, this reproduction of the claim was successful according to the SCORE criteria." Outcome read from Computational: Reproduction results. View paper
