The drunk utilitarian: Blood alcohol concentration predicts utilitarian responses in moral dilemmas

Aaron A. Duke, Laurent Bègue

Cognition, 2015. DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2014.09.006

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 1 replication of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 failed. The earliest on record is from 2021.

Replications

  • The Drunk Utilitarian Revisited: Does Alcohol Really Increase Utilitarianism in Moral Judgment?, Paruzel-Czachura et al. (2021). Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin. Outcome recorded: failed. Despite the use of a substantially larger sample and higher doses of alcohol compared with the ones in prior studies, alcohol had no significant effect on moral judgments. The results pose a challenge to the idea that alcohol increases utilitarianism in moral judgments. Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
