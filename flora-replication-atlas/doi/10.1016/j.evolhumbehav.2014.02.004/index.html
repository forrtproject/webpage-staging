It's the attention that counts: interpersonal attention fosters intimacy and social exchange

Yohsuke Ohtsubo, Asami Matsumura, Chisato Noda, Emiri Sawa, Ayano Yagi, Mana Yamaguchi

Evolution and Human Behavior, 2014. DOI 10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2014.02.004

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications and 1 reproduction of this study. Recorded outcomes: 2 failed, 1 mixed. The earliest on record is from 2019.

Replications

  • Ohtsubo EvoHumanBehavior 2014 zlm2 - Edlund - New Data Replication, Edlund et al. (2019). OSF. Outcome recorded: failed. The H* effect did not replicate as the attention group (M = 21.31, SD=5.40) did not have more intimacy with their partner than the no attention (M = 22.10, SD = 6.41), t (36) = -.410, p =.68, d=-.133. Outcome read from OSF report. View paper
  • Ohtsubo EvoHumanBehavior 2014 zlm2 - Edlund - New Data Replication - 128g6, Parsons et al. (2021). OSF. Outcome recorded: failed. Inconsistent with the hypothesis, intimacy was similar in the attention condition (22.86 ± .6.05) than in the no attention condition (20.85 ± 5.61), t(39) = 1.10, p =.278, d = .344 Outcome read from OSF report. View paper

Reproductions

  • Reproduction (with author data): Ohtsubo et al. (2014, Evolution and Human Behavior), Loomas et al. (2022). OSF. Outcome recorded: computationally reproducible, not checked. Computational: "Based on these criteria, the claim did reproduce." All four criteria (sample size, t-statistic, effect size, p-value) matched original values precisely (N=29, t=5.91, d=2.20, p<.001). Outcome read from Computational: Primary reproduction criteria / Reproduction outcome. View paper
