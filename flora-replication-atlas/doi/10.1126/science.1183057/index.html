Coadministration of a Tumor-Penetrating Peptide Enhances the Efficacy of Cancer Drugs

Kazuki N. Sugahara, Tambet Teesalu, Priya Prakash Karmali, Venkata Ramana Kotamraju, Lilach Agemy, Daniel R. Greenwald, Erkki Ruoslahti

Science, 2010. DOI 10.1126/science.1183057

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 2 failed. The earliest on record is from 2016.

Replications

  • Replication Study: Coadministration of a tumor-penetrating peptide enhances the efficacy of cancer drugs, Mantis et al. (2017). eLife.   • Study 15: Replication of Sugahara et al., 2010 (Science), Iorns et al. (2016). OSF Registries. Outcome recorded: failed. We found that coadministration with iRGD peptide did not have an impact on permeability of the chemotherapeutic agent doxorubicin (DOX) in a xenograft model of prostate cancer, whereas the original study reported that it increased the penetrance of this cancer drug (Figure 2B; Sugahara et al., 2010). Further, in mice bearing orthotopic 22Rv1 human prostate tumors, we did not find a statistically significant difference in tumor weight for mice treated with DOX and iRGD… Outcome read from the abstract.
