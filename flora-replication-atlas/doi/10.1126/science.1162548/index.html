Experiencing Physical Warmth Promotes Interpersonal Warmth

Lawrence E. Williams, John A. Bargh

Science, 2008. DOI 10.1126/science.1162548

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 3 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 3 failed. The earliest on record is from 2014.

Replications

  • Replication of "Experiencing Physical Warmth Promotes Interpersonal Warmth" by, Lynott et al. (2014). Social Psychology. Outcome recorded: failed. We did not replicate the effect in any individual laboratory or when considering the results of the three replications together (total N = 861). Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • No Evidence that Experiencing Physical Warmth Promotes Interpersonal Warmth: Two Failures to Replicate Williams and Bargh (2008), Chabris et al. (2018). Outcome recorded: failed. we attempted to replicate both experiments in field settings with more than triple the sample sizes (128 and 177) and double-blind procedures, but found near-zero effects (r = –.03 and .02). Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • No Evidence that Experiencing Physical Warmth Promotes Interpersonal Warmth: Two Failures to Replicate Williams and Bargh (2008), Chabris et al. (2019). OSF. Outcome recorded: failed. We attempted to replicate both experiments in field settings with more than triple the sample sizes (128 and 177) and double-blind procedures, but found near-zero effects (r = −.03 and .02). In both cases, Bayesian analyses suggest there is substantially more evidence for the null hypothesis of no effect than for the original physical warmth priming hypothesis. Outcome read from the discussion.
