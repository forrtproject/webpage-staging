The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice

Amos Tversky, Daniel Kahneman

Science, 1981. DOI 10.1126/science.7455683

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 4 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 4 successful. The earliest on record is from 2014.

Replications

  • Replication and extension of framing effects to compliance with health behaviors during pandemics, Otterbring et al. (2021). Safety Science. Outcome recorded: successful. we show that the classic Asian Disease Problem (Tversy and Kahneman, 1981) is replicable, regardless of disease type (real vs. hypothetical). Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • Loss aversion, the endowment effect, and gain-loss framing shape preferences for noninstrumental information, Litovsky et al. (2022). Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. Outcome recorded: successful. Study 2 shows that people exhibit the endowment effect for noninstrumental information, and so value information more, simply by virtue of "owning" it. Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • Many Labs 2: Investigating Variation in Replicability Across Samples and Settings, Klein et al. (2018). Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. Outcome recorded: successful. In the aggregate replication sample (N = 7,228), participants were more likely to say that they would go to the other branch when the cheap item was on sale (49%) than when the costly item was on sale (32%; p = 1.01e−50, d = 0.40, 95% CI = [0.35, 0.45]; OR = 2.06, 95% CI = [1.87, 2.27]).
