With a Clean Conscience

Simone Schnall, Jennifer Benton, Sophie Harvey

Psychological Science, 2008. DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02227.x

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 8 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 4 failed, 2 mixed, 2 successful. The earliest on record is from 2013.

Replications

  • Does Cleanliness Influence Moral Judgments?, Johnson et al. (2014). Social Psychology. Outcome recorded: failed. We did not find evidence that physical cleanliness reduced the severity of moral judgments using samples sizes that provided over .99 power to detect the original effect sizes. Our estimates of the overall effect size were much smaller than estimates from Experiment 1 (original d = 0.60, 95% CI [1.23, 0.04], N = 40; replication d = 0.01, 95% CI [0.28, 0.26], N = 208) and Experiment 2 (original d = 0.85, 95% CI [1.47, 0.22], N = 43; replication d = 0.01, 95% CI [.34, 0.36]… Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • Eleven years of student replication projects provide evidence on the correlates of replicability in psychology, Boyce et al. (2023). Royal Society Open Science. Outcome recorded: failed. Subjective replication success rating = 0 (failed). The replication report rated each sub-outcome on a 0-1 scale; the row is recorded as failed only when all sub-outcomes equal 0, successful only when all equal 1, and mixed for any value strictly between. Outcome read from coding note (github.com). View paper
  • Does cleanliness influence moral judgments? Response effort moderates the effect of cleanliness priming on moral judgments, Huang (2014). Frontiers in Psychology. Outcome recorded: mixed. Unlike Study 1 that measured naturally occurring response effort, Study 2 manipulated response effort on the sentence unscrambling task. Support for the cleanliness hypothesis was found among participants instructed to work through the sentence unscrambling task as quickly as possible. In contrast, when participants were instructed to work on the sentence unscrambling task attentively, no support was found for the hypothesized effect of the cleanliness priming. Outcome read from the discussion. View paper
  • Influence of Cleanliness on Moral Judgements: A Direct Replication of Schnall, Benton, and Harvey (2008), Chaves et al. (2018). OSF Registries. Outcome recorded: uninformative. Inconclusive because our results are neither significantly different from the null nor the original. Outcome read from registration: report. View paper
  • Replication of "With a clean conscience: Cleanliness reduces the severity of moral judgments" by Schnall, Benton, and Harvey (2008, Psychological Science), Lee (2013). Outcome recorded: failed. The confirmatory analyses of the current study failed to replicate the findings from the original article. Outcome read from p. 7.
  • Cleanliness primes less severe moral judgments, Besman et al. (2013). Outcome recorded: successful. This result is consistent with the results of the original study. Outcome read from Description of methods/results in template.
  • Clean thoughts lead to less severe moral judgment, Arbesfeld et al. (2014). PsychFileDrawer.org. Outcome recorded: successful. Outcome classified as "Successful Replication" (the project's own label). Outcome read from coding note: abstract.
  • Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science, Collaboration (2015). Science. Outcome recorded: failed. We did not find evidence that physical cleanliness reduced the severity of moral judgments using samples sizes that provided over .99 power to detect the original effect sizes. Our estimates of the overall effect size were significantly smaller than estimates from Experiment 1(original d = -0.61, N = 40; replication d = -0.01, N = 208) and Experiment 2 (original d = -0.85, N = 43; replication d = 0.02, N = 126). Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
