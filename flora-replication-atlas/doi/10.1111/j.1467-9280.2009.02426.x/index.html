Weight as an Embodiment of Importance

Nils B. Jostmann, Daniël Lakens, Thomas W. Schubert

Psychological Science, 2009. DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02426.x

Has this study been replicated?

The atlas records 2 replications of this study. Recorded outcomes: 1 mixed, 1 failed. The earliest on record is from 2015.

Replications

  • Weight as an Embodiment of Importance: Replication and Extensions, Djordjevic & IJzerman (2015). SSRN Electronic Journal. Outcome recorded: mixed. There were no main effect of weight on the importance of having a voice in a decision-making procedure, importance of a book, and importance of healthy eating. However, the present design was slightly different, as the authors included three (counterbalanced) assessments of the effects. When the authors conducted the analysis on the first measurement only in the counterbalanced design, they found the effects on the perceived importance of a book, but with a smaller effect… Outcome read from the abstract. View paper
  • Many Labs 3: Evaluating participant pool quality across the academic semester via replication, Ebersole et al. (2016). Journal of Experimental Social Psychology. Outcome recorded: failed. Across all in-lab replications (N = 2,285), participants in the heavy (M = 6.16, SD = 1.02) and light (M = 6.14, SD = 1.03) clipboard conditions believed that it was similarly important for the university committee to listen to the students' opinions, t(2283) = 0.61, p = .543, d = .03, 95% CI = [−.06, .11]. Outcome read from p. 8. View paper
